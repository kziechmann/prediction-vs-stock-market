How it works

Price bets

  • Markets like "NVDA above $200 on Dec 31" or "TSLA hits $500 in December" are priced a second way from the stock itself: a lognormal model with 20/60-day realized volatility, time counted in trading days.
  • "Hits" markets use the barrier-touch probability; already-touched levels count as 100¢.
  • Edge is measured against the price you would actually pay (ask for YES, 1 − bid for NO), net of Kalshi fees.
  • Realized volatility usually runs below options-implied volatility, especially before earnings. The analyst can override it with implied volatility per ticker.

Event odds

  • For earnings, approvals, deals, lawsuits and leadership markets, the 7-day odds change times an assumed impact gives the move the stock "should" have made.
  • That is compared with the stock's beta-adjusted return vs. SPY over the same week, scaled by the stock's own weekly volatility (σ).
  • Direction and impact start as keyword heuristics; the weekly analyst corrects them, and the corrections persist.
  • Mention/tweet-count markets, popularity rankings and AI leaderboards are excluded as noise.
Not investment advice. Prediction-market prices can be thin, stale or manipulated, and a gap can mean the crowd is wrong rather than the stock. Check each market's resolution rules before trading.

Full methodology

Sector trends first

Markets about an industry-wide development, such as chip export rules, drug pricing or crypto legislation, are netted into one expected move for a sector ETF. They get a ×1.35 boost over single-stock ideas.

Longer horizons first

Scores scale with how far out the market resolves: ×0.45 at a week, ×0.70 at a month, full weight from 90 days.

Unless the gap is very large

A short-dated trade keeps full weight when the contract edge is at least 20 pts or the unpriced stock move is at least 5%.

Methodology

Thresholds shown are the ones this scan used.

What gets scanned

  • Open markets on Polymarket and Kalshi, refreshed every week. Each market gets a confidence from 0 to 1 based on traded volume (about $1K scores 0, $3M scores 1) and resting liquidity or open interest.
  • Markets are linked to about 140 listed companies by name, product, CEO or ticker, and to 15 industry themes, each measured against a sector ETF.
  • Theme and event signals need at least $20K traded. Mention and tweet-count markets, popularity rankings and AI leaderboards are excluded as noise. "Up or down today" and market-cap races follow the price itself, so they're tracked but not scored.

Sector themes

  • Every market in a theme is netted: direction × odds change × assumed impact, summed into one expected move for the theme's ETF. Opposing markets cancel out.
  • That is compared with the ETF's beta-adjusted return vs. SPY over the same window. Both the 7-day and 30-day windows are checked and the larger gap is used, so slow trends show up as well as news shocks.
  • Themes tracked: chip export controls, drug pricing, crypto legislation, cannabis, defense, oil supply, Fannie/Freddie, ACA subsidies, tariffs, EV credits, nuclear power, betting rules, Taiwan risk, AI rules, mortgage rates.

Company events

  • Earnings, approvals, deals, lawsuits, leadership changes and launches: odds change × assumed impact gives the move the stock should have made.
  • Default impacts if YES fully resolves: earnings 4%, takeover target 18%, regulatory 5%, product 2.5%. The analyst replaces these with company-specific estimates.
  • Unpriced gap is the part of that move the stock hasn't made yet. If it already moved further, the gap is zero. If it moved the other way, the gap is capped at the implied move, since that move was probably about something else. Priced in is the share already made.

Share-price bets

  • Markets like "NVDA above $200 on Dec 31" or "TSLA hits $500 in December" are priced a second way from the stock itself: a lognormal model with 20/60-day realized volatility, with time counted in trading days.
  • "Hits" markets use the barrier-touch probability; levels already touched count as 100¢.
  • Edge is measured against the price you would actually pay (ask for YES, 1 − bid for NO), net of Kalshi fees.
  • Realized volatility usually runs below options-implied volatility, especially before earnings. The analyst can substitute implied volatility per ticker.

Ranking

score = base × horizon weight × theme boost

  • Very large deltas keep full weight however soon they resolve: contract edge ≥ 20 pts or unpriced move ≥ 5%.
  • Theme boost ×1.35. A row needs a final score of 0.8 to be listed, and at most 25 are shown.

Analyst review

  • Each week a Claude agent reviews the top candidates. It checks the company or theme link and the market's resolution rules, reads the news behind the odds move, and sets direction and impact.
  • It marks each one Actionable, Watch, Priced in, Noise or Wrong link, and writes the brief above. Its corrections carry over to later weeks.

Track record and limits

  • Every flagged call is saved. Stock and ETF calls are scored by return vs. SPY in the call's direction; contract calls by the change in the contract's price.
  • Impacts are estimates, and a gap can mean the crowd is wrong rather than the stock. Holidays aren't in the trading-day count, and Kalshi volume is counted in contracts.
Not investment advice. Prediction-market prices can be thin, stale or manipulated, and a gap can mean the crowd is wrong rather than the stock. Check each market's resolution rules before trading.
